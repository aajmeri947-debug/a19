The Feynman Technique

Named after the physicist Richard Feynman, famous for explaining complex ideas simply, this four-step method shows you exactly what you do and do not understand.

in this guide

  1. Step one: choose a concept
  2. Step two: teach it to a child
  3. Step three: find the gaps
  4. Step four: simplify and use analogies
  5. Why it works

02.1

Step one: choose a concept

Write the name of the idea at the top of a blank page. Keep it focused: “how vaccines train the immune system” works better than “biology”. Note down what you already know about it.

02.2

Step two: teach it to a child

Explain the idea in plain words, as if to a twelve-year-old. Use short sentences, everyday examples and simple comparisons. Avoid jargon; if you need a technical word, explain it too. Writing it down or saying it aloud both work.

02.3

Step three: find the gaps

Wherever you got stuck, waved your hands or reached for jargon, you have found a gap.

  • Mark each gap clearly.
  • Go back to your notes or textbook.
  • Relearn that part until you can say it simply.

02.4

Step four: simplify and use analogies

Rewrite your explanation, tighter and clearer. Add an analogy that connects the idea to something familiar. Read it aloud. If it flows without stumbling, you understand it. If not, repeat step three.

02.5

Why it works

Explaining forces you to retrieve and organise what you know, which strengthens memory. It also exposes the illusion of understanding, the feeling that you know something just because the text looks familiar.
