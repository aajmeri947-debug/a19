Bloom’s Taxonomy, Explained

In 1956 a group of educators led by Benjamin Bloom sorted learning goals into levels. A revised version from 2001 is still one of the most useful maps of how we think.

  3. Using it to study

01.1

Six levels of thinking

The revised taxonomy lists six levels, often drawn as a pyramid:

  • Remember facts and terms.
  • Understand ideas in your own words.
  • Apply them in new situations.
  • Analyse how parts relate.
  • Evaluate and justify a judgement.
  • Create something new.

01.2

Not a strict ladder

The pyramid shape suggests you must master one level before the next, but real learning moves back and forth. You might create a diagram to help you remember, or analyse a problem to understand it. Think of the levels as different kinds of thinking rather than steps.

01.3

Using it to study

Most revision stays at the remembering level: rereading and highlighting. Push higher by asking yourself questions from each level. Can you explain it to a friend? Use it on a new problem? Compare two ideas? Defend a view? Design an example?

01.4

Verbs that signal each level

Exam questions often reveal their level through the verb. List and define ask you to remember; explain and summarise to understand; solve to apply; compare to analyse; justify to evaluate; design to create.

01.5

A quick exercise

Pick one topic you are studying and write a single question at each of the six levels. Answer them over a few days. Our Bloom’s question builder gives you starters for every level, ready to fill with your own topic.
